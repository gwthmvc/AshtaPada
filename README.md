# How to Play Ashtapada

Ashtapada is an ancient Indian race game that predates chess. The game is played on an 8x8 uncheckered grid, where players race their pieces based on the roll of cowrie shells.

## Components
* **The Board**: An 8x8 grid of 64 identical squares. Certain squares feature an "X" or a special design. These are called **castles** (safe spaces).
* **The Dice**: Traditionally, 4 or 6 cowrie shells are used. The number of shells that land mouth-up determines the move value.
* **The Pieces**: Two to four players each receive a set of distinct pieces (typically 2 to 4 pieces per player).

## Board Setup and Castles
The standard board has specific safe zones marked with an "X":
* The four corner squares.
* The four center squares.
* The two middle squares on each outer edge.

## The Objective
The goal is to move all of your pieces from your starting point, along a spiral path to the center of the board, and successfully exit them before your opponents do.

## Movement and Pathing
1. **Starting Point**: Players enter the board from one of the outer edge castles.
2. **The Path**: pieces travel in a anti-clockwise outer loop around the perimeter of the board, spiraling inward clockwise toward the center squares.
3. **Dice Values**: Roll the cowrie shells to determine your movement points. If using 4 cowrie shells:
    * 1 mouth up = 1 space
    * 2 mouth up = 2 spaces
    * 3 mouth up = 3 spaces
    * 4 mouth up = 4 spaces (plus an extra turn)
    * 0 mouth up (all upside down) = 8 spaces (plus an extra turn)

## Rules of Play
* **Entering Pieces**: A player must roll a specific value (often an 8 or 4 depending on local variations) to enter their first piece onto their starting castle.
* **Capturing**: If a piece lands on a square occupied by an opponent's piece, the opponent's piece is captured and sent back to the starting pool.
* **Safe Zones (Castles)**: pieces resting on a marked castle square cannot be captured. Multiple pieces, even from opposing players, can safely occupy the same castle square.
* **Doubling Up**: If you land two of your own pieces on the same non-castle square, they form a "twin." Twin pieces can move together and cannot be captured by a single piece.

# Ashtapada Dice Rules

Ashtapada dice and cowrie shell rules require repeatedly throwing until a 2 or 3 appears, entering pieces with a 1, and combining values to move.

## Throwing and Turn Mechanics

* **The Roll Sequence:** On your turn, you throw the dice (or cowrie shells) repeatedly and track each result.
* **Ending the Roll:** You keep throwing until you roll a **2 or a 3**, which immediately ends your rolling phase.
* **Using the Throws:** After rolling stops, you use all the recorded numbers from that turn in any order. Every number in the sequence must be used if possible.
* **Starting High Roll:** Players throw once at the start; the highest throw goes first.

## Entering and Moving Pieces

* **Entering the Board:** A new piece can only enter the board using a **1** (placed on your designated starting space).
* **Discarded Rolls:** If you have no pieces on the board, any numbers you throw *before* you roll a 1 are ignored.
* **Combining Values:** You can use numbers individually or add them together to move a single piece forward by the total combined sum.

## Winning the Game
Once a piece reaches the central squares, it must make an exact roll to exit the board. The first player to successfully navigate all of their pieces to the center and exit them wins the game.
